01

An idea worth exploring

During my time at LAU, I worked with a team on FRÉA for L’Oréal Brandstorm. The proposal explored a makeup line designed around a different kind of routine: convenient to wear, considerate of different users, and more conscious of resource use and packaging.

For me, the project was an opportunity to turn broad ambitions into a concept that could be explained. Sustainability and inclusion are large ideas. A competition proposal asks you to connect them to specific product choices and show how those choices fit together.

02

The proposed product experience

At the center of FRÉA was a no-wash-off formula concept: makeup designed to break down overnight, with the aim of reducing the need for water and makeup remover. We also proposed skin-nourishing ingredients intended to replenish the skin during wear.

These were proposed product attributes in a competition concept, rather than a tested formulation or a launched range. The idea gave us a way to explore how convenience and environmental considerations could be discussed together in the same customer experience.

A note I’m carrying forward

An ambitious idea becomes easier to discuss when its choices are specific.

03

Inclusion and the surrounding choices

The proposal included a broad shade range intended to serve diverse users. It also specified recyclable packaging as part of the environmental direction. These choices widened the scope beyond the formula itself: who the product was for, how it was packaged, and how it could fit into a routine.

The concept was presented as adaptable across L’Oréal’s portfolio, spanning makeup brands such as L’Oréal Paris, Maybelline, and Urban Decay, and skincare brands such as Kiehl’s and La Roche-Posay. This was part of the team’s proposal, not a collaboration or adoption by those brands.

04

Learning to connect the pieces

The work combined a product idea with positioning and communication. The challenge was to make the different features read as one coherent concept, rather than a collection of desirable claims.

Looking back, I value the practice of connecting an ambitious idea to specific choices. The concept presentation preserved on LinkedIn captures that stage of learning: taking a question, giving it a shape, and making it understandable enough to discuss with others.
