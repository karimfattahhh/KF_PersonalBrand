01

A broader kind of responsibility

At StratInvest, I worked on a business helping people establish companies in the UAE. The service brought together company registration, residency-related requirements, and the practical questions that come with getting started in another country. My role grew across marketing, sales, the website, and operations.

After tutoring and an early corporate role, this was an opportunity to take responsibility for more of the picture. I worked closely with a mentor, learning how the different parts of a young business depended on each other. A clear message mattered, but so did what happened after someone responded to it.

02

Making the service understandable

I worked on the company’s identity, branding, website structure, and copy. The challenge was to make a complicated service easier to navigate: explain what the business offered, help prospective clients understand the next step, and connect that step to a workable registration process.

My LinkedIn experience also describes building the website with the help of AI tools. What I carry forward from that work is the need to understand the service before deciding how to present it. A page can look finished while the journey behind it still needs work.

A useful website begins with understanding the business behind it.

03

The form was part of the product

The client application form became a central part of registration. I worked on conditional questions in Typeform, where one answer changed which questions appeared next. This required thinking through the logic of different client situations, rather than presenting everyone with the same long questionnaire.

That logic connected the website to the operational work. The information collected had to be useful to the people handling the request. For me, it was a practical lesson in user experience: clarity comes from knowing which question to ask, and when to ask it.

04

Taking the work into the market

I also handled client communications and sales work, including quotations and webinars introducing StratInvest. Those conversations gave the service another test: could I explain the offer clearly enough for someone to understand its relevance to them?

The marketing budget was limited in a competitive industry. I had to connect creative decisions with the reality of reaching customers. Building the website and registration journey was only part of the challenge; the business also needed a sustainable way to generate relevant demand.

05

What stayed with me

When the venture paused, my mentor helped me take the next step toward working in Dubai. The business chapter ended, but the skills did not disappear. Website thinking, onboarding logic, communication, and operational coordination became part of how I approached later work.

I now bring that wider view into education and business development. I am more interested in how all the parts work together, and more aware that the experience someone sees on the surface depends on many decisions underneath it.
